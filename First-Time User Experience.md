You can have the most balanced late-game economy, the most mathematically perfect developer products, and a brilliant server-authoritative architecture (all of those are the blueprints)—but if a player quits in the first three minutes, they will never see any of it.

Here is exactly how the FTUE acts as the engine that powers both your Retention and your Monetization.
[[blueprint for FTUE]]
### 1. FTUE Drives Retention (The 5-Minute Dopamine Hit)

Cold traffic from ads has zero loyalty to your game. They will give you exactly 60 to 180 seconds to convince them not to click back to the Roblox homepage. The FTUE's primary job is to secure that Day 1 (D1) retention by making the core loop immediately satisfying.

- **Eliminating Confusion:** If a player spawns in and doesn't know whether they are supposed to be building a dropper or serving a customer, they leave. The FTUE must aggressively guide them to their first successful action using clean, frictionless UI (like a simple React-Lua prompt pointing directly to the objective).
    
- **Pacing the Core Loop:** The FTUE is where you set the rhythm of the game. If the core loop involves procedural crafting—like mixing ingredients for a brew or preparing a specific coffee order—the first tutorial order should take seconds, not minutes.
    
- **The First Upgrade:** By minute 4, the player must be able to afford their first upgrade (a faster machine, a new ingredient, or a better cauldron). This proves to the player that their time spent is instantly rewarded, locking them in for the rest of the session.
### 2. FTUE Sets Up Monetization (Creating the Need)

You cannot sell a solution if the player hasn't experienced the problem yet. The FTUE subtly teaches the player _why_ your developer products and gamepasses are valuable.

- **Establishing Value:** If you want players to buy a "Time Skip" developer product later, the FTUE has to teach them that waiting is part of the game, but finishing a product is highly rewarding.
    
- **The Starter Pack Placement:** A Starter Pack is useless if offered at minute 1; the player doesn't know if the game is fun yet. The FTUE is designed to build excitement so that when the Starter Pack pops up at minute 5 (right after they complete the tutorial), they are primed and willing to spend 49 Robux to get a massive head start on their next upgrade.
    
- **Building Trust:** Players won't spend Robux on a game that feels broken. A smooth, bug-free FTUE builds the necessary trust for a player to open their wallet.
### The Ultimate Goal of FTUE

Your FTUE is not just a tutorial; it is a sales pitch. It answers the player's subconscious question: _"Is this game worth my time?"_ If the answer is yes, they stay (Lifespan increases). Once they are staying, they get invested, and eventually, they spend (ARPDAU increases).
